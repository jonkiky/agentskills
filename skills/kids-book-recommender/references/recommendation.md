# Recommendation rubric and output

## Ranking

Use qualitative judgments or the following heuristic internally; do not present scores as calibrated probabilities.

| Factor | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Interest, topic and reading goal | 35 |
| Age/content and format fit | 25 |
| Independent reading difficulty or read-aloud fit | 15 |
| Credible recommendation evidence | 20 |
| Discovery value and variety | 5 |

Exclude explicit mismatches before scoring. Missing evidence is unknown, not zero-star quality; seek another source or label the candidate unverified. Never manufacture a numerical reading level. Give professional recommendations more evidentiary weight than sales rankings or isolated reader comments. Include less-famous books where the evidence and fit are strong. Do not incorporate library availability into the quality score; apply it as a final filter only when requested.

## Age and format

- Ages 0-3: board books, repetition, pictures and shared reading.
- Ages 3-5: picture books, rhymes, narrative, read-alouds.
- Ages 5-7: early readers, picture books and beginning chapter books.
- Ages 7-9: early chapter books, comics, illustrated nonfiction and longer read-alouds.
- Ages 9-12: middle grade, graphic novels and nonfiction.
- Ages 12-14: older middle grade or suitable early YA; check themes and content guidance.

These bands guide discovery, not assessments of individual ability. Independent reading and listening comprehension differ. Use actual reading feedback, sample titles and verified publisher/librarian guidance to personalize. Provide content notes when relevant and supported; distinguish publisher target age from assessment.

## Browse output

Start with a short statement of assumed reader preferences. For each book show title/author, format, age guidance, difficulty (comfortable/stretch/read-aloud or verified level), brief hook and personalized reason, then 1-2 direct recommendation/review links and optional metadata/borrowing link. Mark exact holdings as available, checked out, in catalog but status unknown, not found, or unverified. Include date/time and branch only if checked. State which sources were actually accessed and which failed or were skipped. Return fewer strong picks if evidence is insufficient rather than padding the list.

## Acceptance cases

- A 7-year-old who likes animals: select age-matched, evidence-backed books; do not fabricate Lexile.
- A reader who loved The Wild Robot: search similar books; explain similarities and verify that the edition is intended for children.
- Latest books: include publication dates and dated lists; do not claim old lists are current.
- An unavailable library: keep best books and mark holdings unknown; do not invent branch status.
- No API keys: use public curated web sources and disclose any unavailable provider.
- No network: label suggestions provisional; do not claim reviews, current rankings or stock were verified.
